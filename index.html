Electricity, wood & currency rates

The GBP rate is only used to show a £ figure alongside ₹ — it never affects the actual ₹ costing. Each day's entry locks in whatever this rate was when you saved it, so old dates' £ figures stay fixed even if you update the rate later; only new saves pick up a changed rate. "Refresh live rate" pulls the current rate from a public exchange service — review it and click Save settings to apply it.

Labor rates — ₹ per person, applied to the headcount entered in Daily Log

Daily Log now takes a headcount per stage instead of a total ₹ figure — the app multiplies by these rates. Update a rate here if wages change; it applies to every entry saved from then on, same as the other rates.

Monthly overheads — spread across production days automatically

Allocation basis: shared foaming labor, electricity, and daily overhead split between Sheets and Underlay by weight of blocks each line drew from foaming — raw material is charged directly per line, not allocated. Within a line, the allocated share is spread evenly per kg across every SKU produced that day. Say the word if you'd rather allocate by machine-hours per line instead.

Raw materials & opening stock — consumption is now costed by the weighted-average ledger, not a typed rate

MaterialFallback rate ₹/kgOpening stock (kg)Opening stock rate ₹/kg
Opening stock sets the ledger's starting point — the quantity and average cost of each material already sitting in the yard before you started logging purchases here. Fallback rate is only used if a material is consumed with zero purchase history and no opening stock (so the ledger has nothing to base a weighted average on). Once purchases are logged in the Daily Log, the ledger takes over automatically — see the Inventory tab for current stock and cost.

Trim/wastage entered in Daily Log is treated as reclaimed material: it's valued at the current ledger rate of whichever material is named "Rebonded" (matched automatically by name — keep that word in the material's name for this to work), credited back against that line's raw material cost, and added to that material's stock at the same rate — so reused trim isn't double-counted as a pure loss.
